About the project

EvoRoads aims to improve road safety and support the EU’s Vision Zero ambition through a holistic, data-driven Safe System approach. The project develops digital tools and services for dynamic safety assessment, road infrastructure monitoring, predictive maintenance, and proactive risk prevention. By combining digital twins, AI-based analytics, sensor technologies, connectivity, and smart road equipment, EvoRoads will support safer infrastructure and mobility for all road users, including vulnerable road users and connected and automated vehicles. The solutions will be demonstrated in urban and rural environments across several European countries.

Objectives

  • Develop a dynamic road safety assessment framework based on safety criteria, KPIs, and integrated mobility data.
  • Improve road infrastructure monitoring and maintenance through AI, sensor fusion, digital twins, and predictive tools.
  • Enhance safety for all road users through smart infrastructure, proactive warnings, and support for connected and automated mobility.
  • Demonstrate and validate EvoRoads solutions across representative urban and rural environments.
  • Promote large-scale adoption through exploitation, standardisation, policy recommendations, and capacity building.

Publications

  1. Predictive Maintenance of Road Networks via Spatial Clustering and Scheduling: A Data-Driven Approach
    Duc Trung Hoang, Dimitrios Papageorgiou, Peter N. Hansen, Asmus S. Christiansen, and Roberto Galeazzi
    In Proceeding of 6th International Conference on Control and Fault-Tolerant Systems, 2025
    Abs Bib Cite DOI

    Maintenance planning is critical for managing road infrastructure, particularly in densely populated urban areas. This paper presents a data-driven framework that integrates predictive analytics, spatial clustering, and optimization techniques to support more effective maintenance strategies. The proposed approach begins by evaluating individual pavement condition metrics that incorporate threshold-based maintenance triggers. Identified maintenance needs are then grouped into geographically coherent clusters using a density-based clustering algorithm. For each cluster, standardized maintenance probability scores and aggregated cost estimates are computed. A cluster-level optimization model is subsequently formulated to prioritize interventions within budget limits. Experimental results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed approach in enhancing resource allocation and minimizing risk in highpriority zones, enabling more proactive and spatially-informed infrastructure management.
