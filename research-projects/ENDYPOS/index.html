ENDYPOS

Enhanced dynamic positioning for in-harbour navigation via Modelling and Monitoring

2024–2025 Principal Investigator All research projects →

About the project

ENDYPOS aims to enhance the safety, efficiency, and resilience of large-vessel manoeuvring during in-harbour and close-to-coast navigation. The project develops and validates advanced models that capture complex hydrodynamic phenomena, particularly squatting and bank effects, which can significantly affect vessel motion in shallow and restricted waters. Using machine learning, physics-informed modelling, and nonlinear estimation, the project will create models and an assistive early-warning system capable of detecting and predicting these effects and supporting navigators during low-speed manoeuvring. The developed methods will be validated using real ferry operational data and a high-fidelity maritime training simulator.

Objectives

  • Develop accurate, physics-informed vessel models for squatting and bank effects using machine learning and real operational data.
  • Develop an online estimation and early-warning system that detects incipient squat and bank effects and provides decision support to navigators.
  • Validate the developed methods using real vessel data and integration into a high-fidelity training simulator.

Publications

  1. Closed Form Modelling and Identification of Banking Effects in Confined Waters
    Jeppe H. Mikkelsen, Thomas T. Enevoldsen, Bugge T. Jensen, Michael Jeppesen, Roberto Galeazzi, and Dimitrios Papageorgiou
    In Proceedings of 2026 European Control Conference, 2026
    Abs Bib Cite

    Vessels navigating in confined waters are subject to banking effects, which are hydrodynamic forces and moments arising from pressure differentials between the vessel sides, significantly affecting manoeuvrability and safety. Existing numerical approaches such as computational fluid dynamics (CFD) can accurately capture these effects but are computationally expensive and unsuitable for real-time control or estimation. This paper presents a closed-form, first-principles model of banking effects. The model coefficients are identified using physics-informed regression on towing tank experiment data for a scaled container vessel. Validation through Shapley value analysis confirms the significance of the banking terms in reproducing the measured forces and moments. Lastly, the coefficients are shown to be non-dimensional, making the model applicable across different scales that preserve vessel geometry.

    Mikkelsen, Jeppe H.; Enevoldsen, Thomas T.; Jensen, Bugge T.; Jeppesen, Michael; Galeazzi, Roberto; Papageorgiou, Dimitrios (2026). Closed Form Modelling and Identification of Banking Effects in Confined Waters. Proceedings of 2026 European Control Conference.
